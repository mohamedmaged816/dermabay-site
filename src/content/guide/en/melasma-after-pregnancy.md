## What melasma is

Melasma is symmetrical brown or grey-brown patches, usually on the cheeks, forehead, upper lip or chin. It happens when pigment cells become overactive, and the two biggest triggers are **hormones** and **ultraviolet light**. Pregnancy delivers both at once, which is why it's often called the "mask of pregnancy".

## What fades on its own

After delivery, as hormones settle, many women see melasma lighten over 6 to 12 months. This is more likely if you're disciplined with sunscreen from day one. But "lighter" is not "gone". For most women some patches stay, and they darken again every summer without protection.

## The one non-negotiable: sunscreen

Nothing else works if this part is skipped. Melasma responds to even small amounts of daily light, including through car windows and from screens to a lesser degree.

- SPF 50, broad spectrum, every morning, all year.
- A tinted mineral sunscreen with iron oxides blocks visible light too, which matters for melasma.
- Reapply at midday if you're out.
- A hat is not a substitute, but it helps.

## What a dermatologist adds

1. **Diagnosis.** Melasma can look like other pigmentation that needs different treatment. A skin examination sorts it out.
2. **Prescription creams.** Lightening combinations used correctly for a limited period are far more effective than pharmacy whitening products, many of which contain unsafe steroid mixes. If you're breastfeeding, some options are suitable and some aren't; the plan adapts.
3. **Gentle in-clinic treatments.** Light chemical peels and certain mesotherapy approaches speed up lightening without the heat that aggravates melasma.
4. **Maintenance.** Once controlled, a milder cream and sunscreen keep it that way. Stop both and it returns.

## What to avoid

- Aggressive lasers and IPL on melasma. They often worsen it.
- Unlabelled "whitening" creams. Thinning, redness and rebound darkening are common.
- Hormonal contraception without discussing melasma with your doctor; some types feed it.

## Realistic timeline

First visible lightening at 4 to 8 weeks. A solid, stable improvement at 3 to 6 months. Melasma is controlled, not cured, so think of it as a routine rather than a one-off fix.
