## The usual answer: 6 to 8

For most people and most body areas, a full course is **6 to 8 sessions** spaced **4 to 8 weeks apart**. That's roughly a year from first session to final result.

## Why so many

Each hair goes through a growth cycle with three phases: active growth, transition, and resting. The laser destroys the hair root only during active growth, when the root is connected and full of pigment. At any given moment only about a fifth to a third of your hairs are in that phase. Each session clears that group. The next session, 4 to 8 weeks later, catches the next group that has cycled into growth.

That's why hair looks patchy after the first sessions and why the gaps between visits matter. Coming too soon wastes a session on hairs that aren't ready.

## By area

| Area | Typical sessions | Gap between sessions |
|---|---|---|
| Upper lip, chin, face | 8 to 12 | 4 weeks |
| Underarms | 6 to 8 | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Bikini line | 6 to 8 | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Arms | 6 to 8 | 6 weeks |
| Legs | 6 to 8 | 6 to 8 weeks |
| Back and chest (men) | 8 to 10 | 6 to 8 weeks |

## What makes people need more

- **Hormonal areas.** Facial hair in women is often driven by hormones, so it needs more sessions and sometimes a medical check for PCOS.
- **Fine or lighter hair.** Less pigment means the laser has less to target.
- **Skipping sessions.** Long gaps let hair cycles drift and you lose ground.
- **Waxing or plucking between sessions.** That removes the root the laser needs. Shave instead.

## What "permanent" really means

Laser gives long-term hair reduction, typically 70 to 90 percent fewer hairs, with the survivors finer and lighter. Over years a few new hairs can appear, especially on hormonal areas, which is why most people do **one touch-up a year**.
