## What's normal

Hair grows in cycles, and at any time about 10 percent of your hairs are in a resting phase before they shed. That works out to **50 to 100 hairs a day**. You'll see them in the shower drain and your brush. This is not hair loss.

## Red flags: book a visit if…

- Heavy shedding has lasted **more than 8 weeks**.
- Your **parting is getting wider** or your ponytail is noticeably thinner.
- Your **hairline is moving back** at the temples or front.
- You see **bald patches**, round or irregular.
- Your scalp is **itchy, scaly, red or painful**.
- Shedding started after a **new medication**.

## The common causes we see in New Giza

**Telogen effluvium.** Sudden shedding 2 to 3 months after a trigger: illness or fever, surgery, childbirth, a crash diet, severe stress. It feels alarming because you lose hair in handfuls, but it usually recovers over 6 to 12 months once the trigger is gone and the body is supported.

**Low iron stores.** Very common, especially in women. Ferritin can be low even when haemoglobin is normal, which is why a routine blood count misses it.

**Low vitamin D.** Surprisingly common in sunny Egypt because we avoid the sun indoors.

**Thyroid problems.** Both under- and over-active thyroid cause diffuse shedding.

**Pattern hair loss.** Gradual thinning at the crown or widening parting with a family history. It's progressive, which is why starting treatment early matters.

**PCOS.** In women, thinning at the top of the scalp alongside irregular periods, acne or excess facial hair points to a hormonal cause that needs a proper workup.

**Scalp conditions.** Seborrhoeic dermatitis, psoriasis or fungal infections can cause itch, flakes and shedding, and are very treatable.

## What the visit looks like

A detailed history, a scalp examination with a dermatoscope, a gentle pull test, and blood tests when they'll change the plan. You leave with a diagnosis in plain words and a written plan. In-clinic sessions such as PRP are suggested only when they genuinely add something.

## Why early matters

Hair follicles that shrink over years in pattern hair loss eventually stop producing hair at all. Treatment keeps the follicles you have; it cannot restart ones that have closed. The best time to be assessed is when you first notice the change, not after a year of trying shampoos.
